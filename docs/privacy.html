Glass Tab Privacy Policy

Last updated November 18, 2026

Glass Tab is a Chrome extension that gives your new tab page and searches a liquid-glass look. It has no servers, no accounts, no ads and no analytics. Glass Tab does not collect, sell or share your personal information.

What stays on your computer

Everything Glass Tab keeps is stored by Chrome on your computer:

  • Settings and widget content: your wallpaper, clock and layout choices, to-do list, notes, countdown and city. If you're signed in to Chrome with sync on, Chrome syncs these through your Google account like other extension settings.
  • Calendar links: kept on this computer only and never synced, because anyone with the link can read that calendar.
  • To-do app sign-in: if you connect Todoist, Microsoft To Do or Google Tasks, the token that lets Glass Tab reach your list is kept on this computer only and never synced. Disconnecting deletes it.
  • Short-lived copies of your weather, calendar events and, if you connected a to-do app, that list's tasks, so they load instantly. They're refreshed every 15 to 30 minutes.

Glass Tab also shows your most visited sites and recently closed tabs. Chrome provides these to the page directly, and they're never stored by Glass Tab or sent anywhere.

Your location is only used if you click "Use my location", and Chrome asks your permission first. You can turn it off any time in Customize.

To-do apps

Connecting a to-do app is optional. Chrome asks your permission for Glass Tab to reach that app, and Microsoft or Google show their own sign-in window. Glass Tab only asks for access to your tasks (for Google, the Tasks permission alone; for Microsoft, Tasks.ReadWrite). It reads the tasks in the list you pick and sends only the changes you make in the widget. Google user data is used only to show and update your tasks in the widget, is never shared with anyone else, and is never used for ads, in line with the Google API Services User Data Policy.

Glass Google search

If you turn on glass Google search results, Chrome asks your permission for Glass Tab to change google.com. Glass Tab then restyles Google's search results page on your computer. It doesn't read your searches or results, and sends nothing anywhere.

Glass search bar

The search bar you open with the keyboard shortcut is only added to the tab you're on, when you press the shortcut. It can list your open tabs so you can switch to one. That list is made on your computer and never sent anywhere.

Removing your data

Removing Glass Tab from Chrome deletes everything it stored on your computer. Synced settings are removed from your Google account by Chrome sync, or you can clear them in Chrome's sync settings.
